Livestock Welfare

Your animals need more than a fence.

Sheep, goats, pigs and chickens grow old, get dirty and sick, look for shelter from rain and sun, and walk to the water barrel when they're thirsty. Keep them clean, groomed and cared for, and they thrive. Neglect them and they suffer.

FIVE NEEDS

What your animals need

Age

  • Every animal has a lifespan: chickens 2–3 years, pigs 3–4, sheep 3–5, goats 4–6 (in-game years).
  • Old animals stop breeding in the last quarter of their life.
  • Let one die of old age and its carcass gives only 40% of the usual drops. Cull in time.

Cleanliness & health

  • Crowded pens, muck and rot make animals filthy. You can see it: mud spots on the coat and flies around them.
  • Kept animals slowly leave muck on the ground. Shovel it up and you get rot for compost.
  • Filthy animals fall sick, can infect others of their kind, stop healing and lose health.
  • Very sick animals lie down. In clean surroundings they can recover.

Shelter

  • Rain, snow, cold and strong sun stress animals in the open, and stress brings sickness.
  • In bad weather they walk under the nearest roof, and at night they sleep there.
  • A closed barn also keeps out the cold. Wool helps: sheep handle −10 °C, pigs only +2 °C.
  • Strong sun only hurts when it's really hot (26–32 °C depending on the species).

Water

  • Thirst grows over time, faster when it's warm and in the hot sun.
  • Thirsty animals walk to a pond, river or water barrel and drink there.
  • Ponds are free. Barrels and placed buckets really empty (about 8 litres a day for 10 sheep), so refill them.
  • Hot spring water and salt water are not drinkable.

Care

  • Groom with dry grass: sneak and hold right click. The dirt falls off.
  • Pet with an empty hand to calm a stressed animal.
  • Hitting an animal with a shovel never hurts it: it pushes it aside, so you can clean the muck it stands on.

FROM DAWN TO DUSK

A day in the pen

06:00The flock wakes up under the roof and walks out to graze. A thirsty ewe heads straight for the water barrel.
11:00Rain sets in. The sheep walk back under the roof and wait it out.
15:00Muck builds up near the trough. You shovel it out and groom the dirtiest ewe with a bundle of dry grass.
19:00An old ram has stopped breeding. You cull him while he still gives full meat and hide.
22:00Night. Every animal finds a dry spot deep under the roof and lies down to sleep.

FOR SERVERS

Gentler when you need it

Many players asked for a way to play without losing animals. In ModConfig/livestockwelfare.json:

  • AnimalsCanDie: turn death off completely. Animals still get old, sick and weak, but stay at 1 health.
  • OldAgeCanKill, DiseaseCanKill, ExposureCanKill, ThirstCanKill: turn off each cause separately.
  • PauseWhenNoPlayerNearby: no aging or needs while nobody is within 64 blocks, so animals don't suffer while their owner is offline.

Every system (aging, hygiene, weather, thirst, walking to water and shelter) has its own switch and settings.

Compatibility

  • Covers vanilla sheep, goats, pigs and chickens (adults).
  • Petting also triggers Better Husbandry's petting.
  • Animals from genelib mods (e.g. Detailed Animals) may not show mud spots; everything else works.
  • Other mods' animals, horses included, are not covered yet.

How this mod was made

Livestock Welfare is designed and tested by ReginArt and written with the help of an AI assistant, Claude by Anthropic. I'm not a programmer. I came up with the ideas, decided how the animals should behave, and tested every feature in game. The illustrations on this page were also made with AI.

The AI wrote the code, looked up how the base game works (reading the game's own code to follow its rules for rooms, rain and animal AI) and ran automated tests on a test server. Ideas from the Vintage Story community shaped many features, like the safety options for servers.
